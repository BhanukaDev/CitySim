Spline Controls

How a player draws roads, rails, canals, fences and walls with one shared spline tool. Every frame below is a still of the proposed control scheme. Each network type brings its own curve rules. The hands stay the same.

Drawing a road, click by click

A single Draw tool. You click points and every corner rounds itself to the network's corner radius, so three clicks give a smooth bend. There are no separate curve handles to fiddle with. This mode is the default.

Four ways to shape a curve

Keys 1–4 switch mode. Draw covers most roads. Curve is the CS-style mode for exact arcs. Freehand is for organic country lanes and rivers-to-be. Grid lays out city blocks.

Same three clicks, six networks

The clicks are identical in every frame. The network profile decides the result: a street can take a tight corner, a highway sweeps, and a rail line wants more room than the clicks give it, so it takes the largest curve that fits and flags it amber. A fence keeps the hard corner.

These numbers are game-feel values, not engineering standards, and they live in the profile resource, so a mod or a new road type can change them. The player can raise a corner above the default with Shift+wheel. Going below the minimum needs Anarchy (Ctrl+A) and shows red. Rail and highway speed is worked out from the tightest radius on the line, the way Transport Fever 2 does it.

Snapping

Snaps are exact values, so a 90° snap lands on 90.0°, not 89.6°. A small tag says which snap is active, so the tool never feels like it has a mind of its own. Hold Space to draw with no snapping.

Guides to other roads and nodes

Guides show only when you line up with something, at most two at a time, each with a tag saying why. CS2 draws guides too, but players turn them off because they pull roads off the grid. Here guides rank below lots and angles, give exact values, and each type has its own toggle.

Junctions

Junctions appear automatically wherever a new spline ends on, or crosses, an existing one. The profile decides what counts as a legal junction.

Editing after the fact

This is Move It in miniature, built into the game. Press M or click a built spline with the Edit tool. You fix a junction by dragging it, without bulldozing and redrawing.

Parallel and elevation

Parallel draws several networks at once, which suits a road with a canal alongside, twin rail tracks, or a wall along a street. Elevation moves up or down in fixed steps while you draw.

Shaping the ground

When a spline is built, the ground is shaped to the network's cross-section, cut into hills and filled over dips, then blended back to natural ground at the section's slope. It happens once, on build or when an edit drag is released. There's no live preview, so drawing never touches the terrain. The ground stays shaped if the spline is deleted.

CS1 players complain that roads pile up masses of ground instead of climbing hills, and CS2 players that even small slopes reshape whole neighbourhoods. Transport Fever 2 averages bumps into a straight line and falls back to embankments. So how closely a line hugs the ground is a profile setting: streets hug, rails smooth.

What this draws on

What CS1/CS2 and their mods do, and what players say they like or miss.

  • CS2 tool modes: Straight, Simple curve (3 clicks), Complex curve (4), Continuous, Grid, Replace. Parallel offset 0.25–12 cells, elevation steps 1.25 to 10 m. CS2 wiki: Roads
  • CS2 snapping: seven toggles (geometry, 8 m cell length, 90°, building sides, guidelines, zone grid, all). Guides show slope %. CS2 wiki: Snapping · Paradox feature highlight
  • Player complaints: near-miss snaps (179.6° instead of 180°), guide snapping fights curved roads, zone-grid wedges. Paradox forum · Steam thread
  • Precision Engineering (CS1): angle, length and height readouts. Ctrl gives 5° snaps, Shift more info, Alt snaps to guides. GitHub
  • Move It: move, clone and align nodes and curve control points, and fix junctions without bulldozing. Paradox Mods
  • Node Controller: per-node offset, shift, twist, stretch, flatten, bend, crosswalks. Workshop
  • Parallel Road Tool: any networks at set horizontal and vertical offsets with overlay preview. Alt flips side, wheel sets offset. Workshop
  • Network Anarchy / Fine Road Tool: no slope, height or collision limits, sharp junction angles. Network Multitool: straighten, even slope, fillet between roads, arrange in a circle.
  • Road Builder (CS2): assemble custom road cross-sections in one menu and save them for reuse. PCGamesN
  • Guides: CS2 guideline snapping can often break grids if left on, and players disable it with building-side and geometry snaps to keep zoning aligned. CS2 wiki · Steam thread
  • Terrain under roads: CS1 roads build masses of ground underneath instead of following hills, and Network Anarchy's Ground mode is the workaround. CS2 terrain morphing is more extreme and breaks zoning grids. Its retaining walls are an upgrade with bugs at the ends. Network Anarchy comments · Terrain Levelling Is Awful · PC Gamer · Paradox forum
  • Transport Fever 2 terrain: roads follow terrain by default. Bumps are averaged into a straight line, with an embankment, bridge or tunnel when too steep. There are two embankment slopes (a gentle default and a steep fallback when space is tight). TF2 modding wiki · hug terrain thread
  • Transport Fever 2: track tool auto-arcs into existing track and shows the speed limit from curve radius live. TF2 wiki
