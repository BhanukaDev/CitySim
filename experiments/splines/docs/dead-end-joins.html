Dead-end joins

Snapping a new road onto the end of an existing road connects them in the graph, but nothing shows it. This page shows why, three ways to fix it, and how the recommended one plays while drawing.

What's happening now

The two roads do share a node. But a node with only two arms gets no junction shape (footprints start at 3 arms), no label and no tag. So each road is drawn to the node and cut off square: a notch on the outside of the bend, an overlap on the inside. That's the L and the V in your screenshot.

Three ways to fix it

All three are for two arms meeting at a node. Green is the one we picked.

How C plays while drawing

It works like a corner you'd draw in one go, because after the build it is one. The same happens when a draw ends on a dead end.

Decisions (agreed 2026-09-30)

  1. C: continue the road. Snapping onto a same-profile dead end makes the join a normal corner of one road, and every drawing rule applies to it: pending radius, Shift+wheel, clamping (amber), MinRadius (red unless Anarchy), angle snaps against the old road's last leg, Alt for a hard corner, and a near-180° reversal builds hard.
  2. Merging on add happens only for this case (dead end, same profile). Crossings and T's still never merge.
  3. No sharp-angle exception. A 25° V rounds and clamps exactly like a 25° corner drawn in one go.
  4. The old road keeps its shape. Its earlier corners stay at their built radius; only the joined point changes.
  5. Different profiles (street → avenue) keep the node, and the bend is filled. Width tapers come later.
  6. Open: the middle road in the screenshot, whose start sits near the V's end but doesn't touch it. Check whether the node snap missed there.

Where it goes: SplineGraph.AddSpline (continue a dead end instead of joining it), a bend fill for 2-arm nodes in Junctions / RibbonRenderer, a continue tag in the draw preview, and new cases in --demo-junctions.
