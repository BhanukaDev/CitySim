Corner junctions

Snap to a bend's dot, then slide it out toward the bend's corner. The road reshapes live: the curve gets tighter until, at the corner point, it's a sharp corner. Click to put the junction node there, then draw the new road off it at any angle.

What I understood

  1. In every draw mode (Draw, Curve, Freehand, Grid's first click), the cursor snaps to a bend's white dot, as it does now.
  2. While it's snapped, moving the mouse moves a ghost node with it, held on a short track that runs from the dot out to the bend's corner point (where the two straight sides would meet). The track stops there; the ghost can't go anywhere else.
  3. The road previews its new shape as the ghost moves: the bend's radius shrinks so the road passes through the ghost. At the end of the track it's a sharp corner.
  4. Click and the road takes that shape, with a node where the ghost was. The draw starts from that node, so the next click builds a branch at whatever angle you like. The usual angle snaps (square, diagonal, straight on) still help, but nothing forces 90°.
  5. The same works at the end of a leg: draw toward a bend, snap to its dot, slide, click, and the leg ends on the node it made.

This covers both earlier ideas. Slide to the corner point and draw along a side and you get the square T from v1; draw at another angle and you get a Y. It works the same on a 60°, 90° or 135° bend.

Try it

Drag in the map. Before the click the cursor slides the ghost node along its track. Press Click here, then drag again to aim the new road. Pick a bend angle to see it isn't tied to 90°.

Rules

  • The track runs along the bend's centre line of symmetry, from the dot (the radius it has) to the corner point (sharp). The ghost is the cursor's nearest point on it. Moving more than the catch distance off the track lets go of the snap, the same as leaving any snap.
  • Magnets at both ends: the dot (radius unchanged, today's join) and the corner point (a sharp corner).
  • Below the profile's min radius the track turns red: refused unless Anarchy, as with the radius wheel. The corner point itself is fine where the profile allows hard corners (streets do). It's a junction node, not a corner, once the branch is built.
  • Splitting: the road is cut at the node, so each half keeps its share of the reshaped curve (or is straight, at the corner point). The two half-arcs don't get dots of their own; the node is the point. That removes the two extra dots from your screenshot.
  • Undo: the reshape and the first leg are one step. Cancel (RMB / Esc) before building a leg and the bend goes back to how it was.
  • Doesn't change: snapping to a real node, an edge, a dead end, sharp corners (their dot already is the corner point, so the track has length 0), Edit tool behaviour, profiles that don't connect.

Decided

Agreed with the picks below (2026-10-03).

1. Only outward, or inward too?

You said up to the corner point. The track could also run inward from the dot (a bigger, gentler curve), like the Edit tool's radius knob.

My pick: outward only, dot to corner point. Making a bend gentler is the Edit tool's job.

2. Real nodes too?

"Snap to a node, then move it" could also mean sliding an existing junction or dead end before drawing from it.

My pick: bends only. Moving real nodes stays in the Edit tool, so a draw never drags a junction around by accident.

3. A track on long gentle bends?

On a 120° bend the dot is only 2.5 m from the corner point; on a 45° bend it's 26 m. A short track is fiddly when zoomed out.

My pick: keep it, with the two end magnets. Even a 2.5 m track has the two useful stops.

4. Big bends

An avenue bend at R 60 m has its corner point 25 m outside the road. Sliding all the way there swaps a wide sweep for a sharp corner.

My pick: allow it. The ghost shows the new shape before the click, and Ctrl+Z gives it back.
